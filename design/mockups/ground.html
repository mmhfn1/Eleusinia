Common ground

The layout people already know from Discord and Steam, the app's own bars and its lamp-gold, and from the court only the ideas that do a job: a picture made from each key, a seal on every message, a small keep that says why a conversation is private, a slit of light for what is selected and who is talking, and one knife-cut corner on the main button. Ink by night, muted ivory by day.

A. Home

From the first mockup: friends in game first, with the live match and a button, then live calls, then everyone online; the game as each row's second line. From the app: the rail, the column and the two bars under both. From the court: pictures drawn from each key (hover the rail), the selected server lit by a slit of light, the cards' frame stepping in like a portal on hover, the main button's cut corner.

D. A voice channel call

Discord's tiles and the app's voice bar. A shared screen takes the big tile; everyone else sits beside it with their banner (woven from the key when they have no picture). From the court: whoever talks is lit along the tile's edge by the lamp, here moving between people every few seconds, and the same light reaches their name in the channel list.
