The court

A pivot: architecture over game lore. Bofill's colonnades and hemicycles, Pei's knife-edge volumes, Ando's tie holes and slits of light, Isozaki's empty forum, Farrell's fortress, Stirling's coloured rails and Takamatsu's machined Kyoto, through Edo crests and seals and the tapestries of the French court. The backdrop is what the app does: people talking, behind walls only they hold the keys to.

A. Home

Colonnade rail: each server or friend a column topped by its crest; hover lifts it, the selected one is lit by a slit of light. Crests: every key drawn as a mon, so the same person looks the same everywhere and a copycat looks different. Woven banner: the side column's head is a tapestry woven from your key. Portals: friends in game framed like Bofill's arch, the game the far view; hover steps the reveal in. Halls: live calls as small hemicycles, one seat per person. Tie holes: the layout grid, barely there.

B. A conversation, and its keep

Seals: every message carries its author's seal; hover shows it, click opens what it proves. Sending presses the seal like wax. The keep: beside the chat, the conversation's walls drawn as a castle plan with stepped, crenellated walls and a moat (Farrell's fortress at Vauxhall Cross, an Edo castle's baileys): you and Mira inside, the route outside, and who could see what. Hover a fact to light its ring. Sealing for Mira under the box says, while you type, who alone can read it.

C. A call, as a hemicycle

An empty forum (Isozaki's Tsukuba Center leaves its sunken oval empty): the centre of every call is empty, because nothing sits between you. Seats on an arc (Bofill's amphitheatre courtyards): each person a pillar with their weave and crest; whoever talks is lit by a slit of light down the pillar (Ando). The arc grows with the call; a stream takes the stage in the middle. Here the talking moves between people every few seconds, as it would; click a seat to open that person.

D. The pieces

Crest maker: type any name to see its crest and weave; the app draws them from the key, so they cannot be chosen to look like someone else. Type: Bodoni Moda for titles, the type of the French court's last years; Alegreya Sans for everything you read, a humanist sans drawn from calligraphy. Italic serif carries the quiet labels instead of capitals. Palette: ivory and sumi by day, indigo lacquer and ivory by night; vermilion only for seals and what needs you.
