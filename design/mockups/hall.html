The hall

The Steam and Discord layout, with the court let back in where it can move: a call is a row of tall banners, lit where someone talks; profiles change when someone is playing; settings answer every change live; a server's banner can be woven, pictured or taken from a game. Almost everything here can be clicked, hovered, scrolled or typed into. Ink by night, muted ivory by day.

A. Home

From the first mockup: friends in game first, with the live match and a button, then live calls, then everyone online; the game as each row's second line. From the app: the rail, the column and the two bars under both. From the court: pictures built from each key (hover the rail), the selected server lit by a slit of light, the cards' frame stepping in like a portal on hover, the main button's cut corner.

D. A voice channel call, as banners

The court's call, made quieter: everyone stands as a tall banner made from their key, side by side like an arcade. Whoever talks has their banner brighten and their outline lit, and a thin seam of light runs down from the banner's top, longer as they talk louder (here a stand-in voice). When someone shares, click Watch on their banner: the stream takes the stage and the banners step down into a row beneath it, still lighting up.

F. Settings

Discord's settings shape with a live preview that answers every change. My profile: type a name or an about line, choose how your banner is woven from your key (or upload one, or let the game's art take over while you play) and shift its tones; the preview, and your banner in the call above, follow. Appearance: the theme cards switch the whole page, and Reduce motion stills every seam. Voice: talk (a stand-in voice) and watch your banner light up past the sensitivity you set. Activity: each switch takes a detail off what friends see.
