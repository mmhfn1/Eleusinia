# Eleusinia

Peer-to-peer chat and voice calls for desktop with no central server: a Discord/TeamSpeak-style app
where every message and call goes directly between the people involved. Electron + React + TypeScript.
See `README.md` for features, known limits and the roadmap.

## Architecture

| Path | Role |
|---|---|
| `src/main/index.ts` | Electron main: window, identity (ed25519 key, encrypted with `safeStorage` when available; one that cannot be unlocked, such as a data folder copied from another computer, offers a new identity and keeps the old file as `identity-unreadable-<time>.json`; the data folder is owner-only, `privateDir`), and one IPC handler `call(method, ...args)` that forwards to the node. Node events wait until the page's first `state` call (held again only on a real reload, at most 10 s), so a call joined before the window listened is not lost. Update checks are triggered by events, never two at once: a contact on a newer version than ours (`app.getVersion()`, at most one check per 10 min, `contactCheck`, so a wrong or invented version can neither spam nor block checks), a call that fails to connect within 5 s, and an hourly fallback. The start's check (`startUpdates`, answered within 6 s or taken as offline) reports to `node.updateStep` (main only, `MAIN_ONLY`); a newer release it finds is downloaded behind the splash, with its progress there (`holding`), then installed (`installNow`, also the window's `installUpdate`) and the new version opens and signs in by itself; one found after the splash closed (`node.starting()`) is offered in the window as before. `appMemoryMb` (the `getAppMetrics` sum) feeds both `meta` and the startup checks |
| Startup checks (`src/shared/startup.ts`, `node.ts` `startup()`, `src/renderer/Splash.tsx`, `splash.css`) | The window opens on a splash (the emblem as a lamp breathing against cold fog, 2.6 s, only opacity and transform; still under reduced motion; the title bar's buttons stay usable on top) that closes by itself, never on a click, once every check is done: account, network (on the DHT), updates (answered; a newer release is installed before the app opens: the step says "Updating Eleusinia" with the download's progress, and while it holds the checks do not give up, up to 5 minutes, `HOLD_MS`), links (every friend and `linkPick` pick linked, or its first dial definitely failed; a relay attempt that follows is not waited on, as it held the splash ~14.5 s in 0.1.3), presence (heard from a mate, no card fetch left), catch-up (each greeting's first `have`/`sops` answered and held, `servers.greet(k, true)`, `behind()`; `greet` asks only where both sides are members, so a group we left is never waited on), memory (one diagnostics line with the time and MB; `redial` already prunes), and in the window the first state and the device list. `node.ts` emits `{ type: 'startup' }` at most every 200 ms until ready or `startupMs` (20 s), which gives up with `pending` ("Still connecting to Bob"; the give-up diagnostics line names each catch-up still waited on), shown as a toast after the splash (`StillConnecting`). `window.api.call('startup')` returns the latest. On joining the DHT, `redial` and `nameUnlinked` (5 at a time) run at once. Tests: `test/startup.test.ts` and the startup tests in `test/node.test.ts`; the smoke run logs each launch's startup event and requires `ready` within 15 s on Ann's restarts |
| `src/main/node.ts` | Friends (requests go both over a direct link and, for peers that can never link, as announcements on `requestTopic(key)` checked every 30 s, with each profile published as a signed DHT record), groups, message logs and their sync, settings, call signaling (`signal`, `relayOpen`, `relayClose`). Logs stay on disk with as many recently used conversations in memory as fit in 50,000 messages (`keepMsgs`; the one in use always stays); `history(conv, limit)` returns the newest messages, from a time-sorted copy made once per change (`sorted`). History after a key theft: `confirmed` keeps, per conversation and peer, the most of each author's messages that peer ever confirmed, and nothing at or below it is sent to that peer again (kept out of state.json in `confirmed/<conv>.jsonl`: a receipt appends `{ k, h }` with only the authors it raised, `writeConfirmed` compacts each file at start and after a prune, an older state.json's copy moves there at start); a peer asking below it is told (`held`) and writes a `{ skip }` line to its log so new messages still arrive (`heldFrom` in sync.ts accepts that only for the sender's own messages and ours, so no member can make us drop someone else's). Our own numbering carries on after `acked` (what friends confirmed of ours, `acked`, kept in state.json) when the log holds less, so a message written after losing the logs is not taken for one they already have. A friend who tells us over a link that they removed us is noted in `unfriended`, and their DHT acceptances, which outlive the removal by up to ~20 min, are not believed for 30 min (`STALE_ACCEPT`). A group message, and every 5 s our receipt for it (`receipts`), goes on to only 3 random linked members (`forwardTo` for messages, 0 in the test that proves asking works), not all of them; a member it missed sees from someone's receipt that it lacks something and asks (at most every 5 s per peer, `askedFor`). `changed()` saves through `saveSoon` (within 2 s, flushed by `destroy`) and pushes through `pushSoon` (100 ms), so a burst of changes is one write and one state push; a greeting with an unchanged profile writes nothing. A group change received is passed on to 3 random linked members, like messages, not to everyone. Bounded links: we dial friends, whoever we have something for (`demand`: a call signal addressed to them, a file only they have, `files.want`) and per group `groupLinks` (8) members picked by a hash of both keys (`linkPick`, further down the list only past picks whose dial failed, at most 24 tried, so a burst of first dials stays at 8), not every member; a link we dialed and no longer need closes after 10 min (`closeIdle`: never friends, picks, a call's relay port, a file transfer). Members we are not linked with are known online from signed presence notes (`src/main/presence.ts`, `createPresence`; `signPresence`/`verifyPresence` in sync.ts; also sent within 2 s when a group reaches us or gains someone, `announceSoon`; ours every minute and on joining or leaving a group call, `ourCall`; passed on to 3 linked members sharing a group with the author; kept 3 min, `State.present`; at most every 20 s, every 10 s when the profile changed, every 250 ms when the call changed; dropped at once when our own link with them closes, `gone`; every greeting carries a snapshot of ours and the live notes we hold of people we share a group or server with, `snapshot`, so a fresh copy learns who is in which call from its first link; a newer note's call always counts, linked or not, since a linked person's join may never come); each note carries the author's `profileVersion`, and a member not linked with us whose version differs from ours (`profileV`, set from greetings and cards) has their card fetched again (`refreshCard`: a new version at most every 10 s, an unchanged card retried 3 times 10 s apart, since the new one may not be on the DHT yet); a note's call reaches the page as that member's `join`/`leave` (`callMoved`), and call.ts answers someone in its call without a known version with a `join` addressed to them, which makes the node dial them; joining a call also sends `join` to each person already in it who is not online for us (not linked). The smoke run starts every window with `ELEUSINIA_TEST_GROUP_LINKS=0` (honoured only on a local test DHT), so Bob and Cy, not friends, never link and their group call goes through exactly that. Pictures live apart in `pictures/<id>` (named by hash; state.json holds `pic:<id>`, `toRef`/`fromRef`, unreferenced ones deleted at start) and reach the window as `eleusinia-pic://<id>` (`picUrl` in `state()`, served by index.ts through `picture`). Catch-ups go a page at a time (`CATCHUP_PAGE` 200, none while 1 MB is unsent on the link; `sentUpTo` holds back only receipts, for 10 s); a message counts as unread only if written after the group reached us (`joinedAt`) or after our join to the channel's server (`since`); `State.chats` carries server channels too (`${server}.${channel}`), which the rail lists under their server, not as DMs; a group that reached us after this change takes a shallow start (`shallow`: our `have` says `tail`, and a member holding more than `SHALLOW` (100) of an author we hold nothing of starts us at their newest 100; that page's first message of each such author says `start`, and only that one is taken past a gap, logged as a `{ tail }` line: a message that merely overtook earlier ones, as when everyone writes at once, is never a start); a torn last line (a write cut short) is ended with a line break when the log is first read (`msgsOf`), so the next message is not glued to it, and older history comes on request (`older`: a page at or below each author's tail, each author's run by number, newest first, merged by time; one step down per message, `history().earlier` while any is left; members answer one request a second); a message's time must be a whole number a Date can hold (`verifyMsg`) and is turned away while more than 10 min ahead (`EDIT_AHEAD_MS`); each author's messages per conversation pass a bucket (`FLOOD`: 400, then 20 a second), past which they are turned away and asked for again 2 s later (`floodRetry`). `inGroupWith` is one cached set, rebuilt when a group's member list changes. Every redial, `prune` drops profiles of people we share nothing with (friends, group members, those who left or were removed from our groups, and blocked people stay) and what non-members confirmed (`confirmed`), and clears memory-only per-peer records (`used`, `signalled`, `versions`, long-refilled `flood` buckets). Friend requests are announced with `announceRequest` (sealed, `src/shared/sealed.ts`) before the dial that rings, and resolved with `senderOf` (each look resolves at most 20 unknown keys, picked at random and in parallel, so junk keys on the topic cannot hold real requests back); search reads only published cards (`fetchCard`), which point to a thumbnail record under 1 KB made by `thumbnail` (index.ts, `nativeImage`); the exact friend ID is no longer announced (presence), username prefixes only with `settings.suggest`. **No Electron imports**, so tests run it directly |
| `src/main/network.ts` | One encrypted HyperDHT stream per peer, found by public key. Newline-delimited JSON `Wire` messages; `packet()` sends call datagrams (≤1000 B unreliable, larger over the stream). `dial(key, via)` links through a contact's blind relay (hyperdht `relayThrough`); `setRelay` runs ours (`blind-relay`'s server on a second key, `relayKeyPair`), accepting only our contacts and capped by `RELAY_LIMITS`. The DHT gets our identity as its default key so hyperdht's own relay dials show who we are. The server's firewall asks `admit` (node.ts: friends, group members, people we asked) before the handshake completes, so strangers never learn our address; a turned-away dial is reported as `knock`, which node.ts uses as a doorbell (`ring`: look for requests now; a dial from someone we asked looks for their acceptance now and again 3 s later). A dialed link that has received no bytes 3 s after `open` is dropped and dialed once more at once: hyperdht reports `open` before the other side answers, and that side may already have dropped the stream. Both sides dialing at once is told apart from a restarted peer by a 3 s window (`raceMs`): inside it the stream dialed by the smaller key wins, past it the newer stream replaces the old. `strict` is the test-only strict-router simulation |
| Relaying through a mutual friend (`node.ts`) | Settings.relay, on unless set to false (`relay !== false`), is only relaying for others: anyone can be reached through a relaying friend (`hello.via`, from 0.0.45; older copies accept relayed links only while relaying themselves). A contact's `hello.relay` says it relays (old versions never send it). For a peer with something pending (`demand`: unconfirmed messages, an open conversation, a call signal) whose direct dial failed, ask up to 6 relaying contacts `reach` (`pickRelays` in `src/shared/relaypick.ts`: the relay that worked last, then peers in the target's call by presence notes, then those sharing a server or group with them, shuffled within each rank); dial through the first yes, waiting up to 12 s. A relay asked for a contact it is not linked with links to them first (`relayFor`: up to 10 s, at most 4 such links for others at a time) instead of refusing, which is what most 0.1.3 refusals were. A contact whose dial reaches us while unlinked (`incoming` in network.ts, from the firewall) is dialed back after 10 s (`REACH_DIAL_MS`, so it does not race their own hole punch, which can take 8 to 9 s, and replace the link it forms), at most every 10 s, since a router that turns their dial away often lets ours out (`design/plan-relay-anyone.md`; tested in `test/node.test.ts`). `RELAY_LIMITS` (network.ts) is 8 pairs, 4 per contact, 24 KB/s a direction per pair. `State.unreachable`: people whose direct dial and a round of relay asks both failed (`relayFailed` pushes it); the window (`reachOf`, `REACH_WORDS`, `pendingWords` in `src/shared/reach.ts`, tested in `test/reach.test.ts`) shows someone seen online through presence notes but not linked as "Online, connecting…", or "Online, can't connect yet" once unreachable, in the friends list (counted under Online), the profile card and a DM's pending messages, and a call tile still forming as "Can't connect yet" (`callWords`, with `STATE_WORDS`, also in reach.ts). The dialer sends `relayed`; `State.relayed` makes call.ts send voice only and `probe` skip them. Only friends (and whoever a call signal is addressed to) are sought out this way (`need`), never every group member. Backoff per peer, also when nobody could relay yet, reset when a new relaying contact appears; redials back off too after 3 quick tries while relaying (`due`); a catch-up sends each message once per link (`sentUpTo`, cleared when the link closes); a peer's "you already had these" claim about our own messages is believed only up to `acked` (`heldFrom`); a relay going offline drops and retries its pairs at once; idle relayed links close after 10 min. Group members never linked with are named from their published card (`nameUnlinked`, each at most hourly). Tests: the `strict` tests in `test/node.test.ts` |
| `src/main/files.ts` | Shared files (no Electron imports). A `Msg` may carry `files` (`FileRef`: sha256, name, size, type, tiny preview; validated and signed in `src/shared/media.ts`/`sync.ts`); the bytes are pulled by hash in 64 KB `get`/`part` pieces over the peer link, 4 in flight, from a member of that conversation (the sender first), resumed from `files/<h>.part` and checked against the hash. Serves only members of a conversation the file is in, answering `nope` the same way to a guess. Pictures ≤25 MB fetch by themselves, the rest on request to a path from the save dialog; relayed links carry only those pictures. Sent files are referenced where they are (`addPath`), not copied; JPEG/PNG are re-encoded without metadata in `src/main/index.ts` (`attach`), which also serves checked files to the page as `eleusinia-file://<hash>` (inline types only). Tests: `test/files.test.ts` and the files test in `test/node.test.ts` |
| Activity (`src/shared/activity.ts`, `src/shared/steam.ts`, `src/main/activity.ts`, `src/renderer/Activity.tsx`) | What someone is playing, shared with friends and group members only, on unless Settings → Activity turns it off (`sharing`), each detail behind its own switch (`shareable`; the store details, genre, developer, release date and description, are off unless turned on); people can be hidden one by one. Plan and status: `docs/roadmap-activity.md`. `main/activity.ts` checks every 10 s: Steam's `RunningAppID` (Windows registry via `reg query`, `registry.vdf` elsewhere), then the running programs against `KNOWN_GAMES` and the user's own (`extra`). For a Steam game: the name from its `appmanifest`, playtime from `localconfig.vdf`, achievements from Steam's binary stats files (`achievementsOf`), genre, developer and description from the public store endpoint, rarity from the public global stats endpoint. Sent as `{ t: 'activity' }`, checked by `cleanActivity` (times moved onto our clock), memory only (`State.activity`, ours under our own key as friends see it). Fetches from Steam, Bungie.net and the art cache time out (10 to 15 s, `AbortSignal.timeout` in index.ts), so a hung request cannot stall the watcher. Peer numbers and text are clamped by `src/shared/clean.ts` (`num`, `str`). Pictures come only through `eleusinia-art://`, built from ids by `artUrl` (Steam's CDN), fetched by main and cached in `art/`. `ELEUSINIA_TEST_ACTIVITY` (a game as JSON) stands in for the watcher in the smoke run. CS2 and Dota 2: Valve's Game State Integration (`src/main/gsi.ts`, one server for both, told apart by `provider.appid`): Settings → Activity → the Steam tile's switch per game (`Connections` in `Activity.tsx`; the Bungie.net tile beside it) writes `gamestate_integration_eleusinia.cfg` into the game's `cfg` folder (Dota: `cfg/gamestate_integration/`, found through `steamAppDir`; Dota also needs the `-gamestateintegration` launch option, which Settings checks in `localconfig.vdf` via `steamLaunchOptions` and only explains, never sets), and the game POSTs to a server on 127.0.0.1 with the port and token kept in `gsi.json` (a taken or blocked port is swapped and the files rewritten; `status` says when each game was last heard and why posts were turned away, shown in the Steam panel); a game sending details counts as running even before the watcher sees it; `dotaOf` (`src/shared/dota.ts`) turns a Dota payload into `Game.dota` (hero, level, score, clock, stats; gold, items, health and buyback only with `DotaSettings.live`; a spectated match gives only the match); `cs2Of` turns a payload into `Game.cs2` (map, mode, score, round results, the phase clock as an end time, our stats and state; a watched player's numbers are never taken for ours), merged onto the Steam game in `index.ts` `startActivity` and dropped after 30 s of silence. Live health, money and weapon are shared only when turned on (`Cs2Settings.state`), since they would help an opponent. `ELEUSINIA_TEST_GSI_CFG` (one subfolder per game) stands in for the cfg folders in the smoke run. Destiny 2 (`src/main/bungie.ts`, `src/shared/destiny.ts`): the Bungie.net account is linked in the user's browser (bungie.net, then the return page in the mmhfn1.github.io repository, then a server on 127.0.0.1 for that one sign-in, its port in `state`); a confidential client, so a 90-day refresh token; the accounts come from the public `GetMembershipsById` (no extra scope) and nothing is kept until they are known; sealed with `safeStorage` in `bungie.json`; `GetProfile` every 5 s only while Destiny 2 is the game; Manifest definitions one by one (`neededDefs`, then `destinyOf`), cached in `bungie-defs.json` per Manifest version; pictures only from bungie.net's Destiny icon and activity picture folders (`BUNGIE_PATH`). `ELEUSINIA_TEST_BUNGIE` (a stand-in's address) makes linking approve at once in the smoke run. Marathon has no API yet: Steam only |
| `src/main/inbox.ts` | The developers' inbox (`npm run inbox`, which is the app started with `--inbox`): receives reports from anyone over the DHT, rate-limits (`reportLimit`: 20 an hour per sender and 100 an hour in all, since keys cost nothing), saves them and files GitHub issues with a token that only lives on that machine. Log pulls (`npm run logs`, the app started with `--inbox --pull`, handed to a running inbox through `second-instance`): `inbox.pull(to)` publishes a `Pull` (`shared/reports.ts`: `pullTargets`, `cleanPull`, `pullFor`) under the inbox's own key; tester builds (`BUILD`) read it with `net.fetch(inbox, true)` on joining the DHT and every 2 min (`checkPull` in node.ts, unless `settings.shareLogs` is false), remember answered ones in `State.pulled`, and answer once through `pullLogs` (index.ts: a `logs` report with the diagnostics, its id derived from the pull and our key); `logs` reports are not filed one by one: the inbox keeps each pull and its answers in `pulls.json` (matched by the answer's title, "Logs for pull <6 hex>") and files one issue per pull `gatherMs` (10 min) after it (`pullIssueOf`, a `pullSection` per tester sharing the size limit), later answers as comments on it. Also, a failed dial's hyperdht error code is logged once per peer and reason (`failed(key, via, why)`). See `docs/reports.md` |
| `src/shared/reports.ts` | Problem and crash reports: the scrubber every diagnostic line passes (`scrub`), report validation (`cleanReport`), the in-memory diagnostics (`createDiagnostics`) and the inbox key (`INBOX_KEY`). The main process keeps the diagnostics and sends crash reports; `node.ts` queues reports in `outbox/` until the inbox confirms them |
| `src/main/relay.ts` | Fallback when routers block direct WebRTC: a local UDP port offered to WebRTC as a low-priority `relay` candidate, with packets ferried over the peer link. Media stays DTLS-SRTP encrypted |
| `src/shared/protocol.ts` | Wire, state and event types; validation (`isKey`, `cleanProfile`, …); message stacking |
| `src/shared/sync.ts` | Signing and verification, group merge, missing-message calculation. Groups: the owner is proven by the id (`groupIdOf(owner, nonce)`), or for groups from before owners found once from the stored copy (`ownerOf`, on load in `node.ts`); members are those reachable from the owner through signed adds, minus signed leaves and the owner's signed removals (`signRemoved`); name and picture changes are signed (`signEdit`) and ignored more than `EDIT_AHEAD_MS` ahead. Without a known owner the old rules apply (every listed member counts) |
| `src/shared/server.ts`, `test/server.test.ts` | Servers, phase 1 step 1 (`docs/roadmap-servers.md`): a server is a log of signed ops (`signOp`/`verifyOp`, per-author `seq` with no gaps, Lamport `n`), replayed in one order (n, author, seq) by `replay`, which accepts each op only if its author had the right at that point, so the same ops give the same server in any arrival order. The id is proven by the `create` op (`serverIdOf(owner, nonce)`); roles are ordered (index = rank, `@everyone` at 0 with the server id) and nobody acts at or above their own highest role or grants what they lack; `permsOf`/`can` give channel permissions Discord-style (@everyone override, then the member's roles'); joins are written by a member with the joiner's proof over the invite key (`inviteKeyPair`, `joinProof`). Key ideas worth keeping intact:

- A `Key` is a 64-hex ed25519 public key; it is the identity and the address on the DHT.
- Everything from another peer is untrusted: validate and cap it (see `cleanProfile`, `validGroup`, `verifyMsg`).
- Messages and group changes are signed by their author; a member's leave can only be signed by themselves.
- `CALL_VERSION` (`src/shared/protocol.ts`) is only logged: calls never wait on versions. A change older copies cannot follow in a call (slot order, signal meanings) must therefore stay compatible, or wait until everyone has updated. Every push to `main` reaches all testers as an update.
- The installed app updates itself from GitHub Releases (`startUpdates` in `src/main/index.ts`); `ELEUSINIA_UPDATE_URL` points it at another folder of releases for testing, `ELEUSINIA_NO_UPDATES=1` turns it off (the smoke run does).
- Tie-breaks use key order: when both sides dial or offer at once, the smaller key wins.
- Call signals are processed one at a time (`onSignal` queue) so ICE candidates never overtake their offer.
- Every local test DHT (smoke run, `test/node.test.ts`, `test/inbox.test.ts`) has 10 nodes on purpose: with 3, hyperdht's NAT check on the dialed copy often lacks samples, it aborts the hole punch it races against a LAN shortcut, and the dialer's link, already reported open, dies silently ~13 s later (see the comment in `test/smoke.mjs`); on the slower GitHub runner it also failed release checks.

## Conventions

- No semicolons, single quotes, 2-space indent, trailing commas in multi-line literals.
- Short functions; comments are brief and explain *why*, not what.
- Keep `node.ts` free of Electron and DOM imports; put pure logic in `src/shared/` so it can be unit tested.
- Prefer no new runtime dependencies; native modules need a good reason (they complicate packaging).
- Update `README.md` when a feature, limit or command changes.
